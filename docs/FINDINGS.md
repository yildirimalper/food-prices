# Who collects the rent? Türkiye's farm-to-shelf gap, 2026

*All figures September 2026 unless stated. Annual = change on the same month a
year earlier. YTD = change since December 2025. Sources in `README.md`;
every curated figure carries its source in `data/curated/`.*

---

## 1. The puzzle, restated with numbers

| | 2026 |
|---|---|
| Fruit production | **+60.8%** (≈31.5 mn t) |
| Vegetable production | **−0.8%** (≈33 mn t) |
| Food & non-alcoholic beverages CPI, October | **+27.7%** y/y |
| Headline CPI, October | **+29.8%** y/y |

Food inflation is running *below* headline. It is no longer the leader — housing
and utilities (+40.2%) and transport (+36.4%) are both well above it. That is the
first clue: whatever is going on is not specific to food.

## 2. The farm gate is the slowest link in the chain

Cumulative price change since December 2025:

| Stage | YTD | Annual |
|---|---|---|
| Farm gate (Tarım-ÜFE) | **+7.0%** | +12.9% |
| Food manufacturing (PPI, NACE C10) | +20.4% | +24.9% |
| Retail food (HICP CP011) | +21.2% | +26.9% |
| Eating out (HICP CP111) | +24.7% | +30.8% |

The shelf has risen three times as fast as the field this year. But note *where*
the gap opens: almost all of it (13.4 of the 14.2 points) appears between the farm
gate and the **factory** gate. Between factory and shelf there is only 0.8 points.
Whatever is happening, in 2026 it is not mainly the supermarket.

## 3. The farmer is squeezed from both sides

| | Annual |
|---|---|
| Farm **output** prices (Tarım-ÜFE, Sep) | +12.9% |
| Farm **input** prices (Tarım-GFE, Aug) | **+30.3%** |
| — energy & lubricants | +43.1% |
| — fertiliser & soil improvers | +28.8% |
| **Terms of trade** | **−13.3%** |

A farmer selling the same tonnage in 2026 as in 2025 earns 13% less in real
purchasing power over their own inputs. This is the single clearest finding in the
data: **the farmer's loss is real and large, and it is not mainly a transfer to the
consumer, because the consumer is paying more too.**

## 4. Where the money actually goes

Annual % change, every link priced on the same basis:

| | Annual |
|---|---|
| Crude oil, in lira | **+102.2%** |
| Wheat (world), in lira | +74.7% |
| Soybean meal (feed), in lira | +47.0% |
| Farm energy & lubricants | +43.1% |
| Maize (feed), in lira | +42.9% |
| **Dairy processing (PPI)** | **+42.5%** |
| Housing rents | +41.3% |
| Household electricity & gas | +41.0% |
| Transport | +35.0% |
| Beverage manufacturing | +32.7% |
| Energy (producer prices) | +30.8% |
| Restaurants & cafés | +30.8% |
| Plastic packaging | +29.2% |
| Meat processing | +28.7% |
| Statutory minimum wage | +27.0% |
| **Retail food** | **+26.9%** |
| Paper & board packaging | +25.9% |
| Grain milling | +25.5% |
| Bakery | +24.0% |
| Lira depreciation vs USD | +17.7% |
| **Farm-gate prices** | **+12.9%** |

Retail food at +26.9% sits in the *middle* of this list, below the minimum wage,
below packaging, below rent, below energy, below every imported input measured in
lira. The farm gate sits at the bottom, alone.

**So the honest answer to "who collects the rent" is mostly: nobody in the food
chain.** The chain is passing through a cost shock that originates in energy, the
exchange rate, imported feed and grain, administered utility prices, commercial
rent and wages. Food retail is not where the margin is being made — it is roughly
tracking its own costs.

## 5. …with two exceptions the data does flag

Comparing each stage with the stage before it:

| Chain | Processing | Retail | Retail − processing |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Meat** | +28.7% | +37.8% | **+9.1 pp** |
| **Dairy & eggs** | **+42.5%** | +32.0% | −10.5 pp |
| Bread & cereals | +25.5% | +23.8% | −1.7 pp |
| Oils & fats | +29.0% | +27.5% | −1.6 pp |
| Non-alc beverages | +32.7% | +34.9% | +2.3 pp |
| Vegetables | *farm* +61.1% | +39.6% | −21.4 pp |

Two margins are visibly widening:

- **Meat at the retail/butcher stage** — shelf prices are running 9 points ahead of
  processor prices.
- **Dairy at the processing stage** — processor prices at +42.5% are the fastest
  number anywhere in the domestic food chain, ahead of both raw milk and retail
  dairy (+32.0%).

Both have independent corroboration. Türkiye's Competition Authority fined BİM,
CarrefourSA, Migros, A101 and Şok a combined **₺2.6bn** for Article 4 violations,
and has an open investigation into **50+ firms plus ASÜD** covering raw-milk
purchasing, dairy-feed sales and packaged dairy — including an interim measure over
producers being pushed to buy particular feed, a finding that the feed-to-milk
parity was not honoured, and that payments to producers were delayed to their
disadvantage. The data flagged dairy processing before the regulator's docket was
consulted; they agree.

## 6. The fruit harvest did work — that is why you cannot see it

Food inflation decomposed into its sub-groups (share of the food basket × that
group's annual rate):

| Group | Share of food basket | Annual | Contribution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Meat | 18.9% | +37.8% | **7.12 pp** |
| Vegetables & pulses | 14.1% | +39.6% | **5.58 pp** |
| Cereals & bakery | 18.2% | +23.8% | 4.33 pp |
| Milk, dairy & eggs | 13.4% | +32.0% | 4.29 pp |
| Non-alcoholic beverages | 9.6% | +34.9% | 3.34 pp |
| Oils & fats | 5.7% | +27.5% | 1.57 pp |
| Sugar & confectionery | 6.2% | +19.3% | 1.20 pp |
| Ready-made & other | 3.0% | +27.8% | 0.83 pp |
| Fish & seafood | 1.6% | +4.8% | 0.07 pp |
| **Fruit & nuts** | **9.4%** | **−1.0%** | **−0.09 pp** |

Fruit is the **only deflating category in the entire food basket**, and it is
*subtracting* from food inflation. The bumper harvest was passed through to
consumers essentially in full.

Had fruit merely tracked the rest of the basket, food inflation would have been
**31.3%** instead of **28.3%**. The harvest was worth about **3 percentage points**
of food inflation to Turkish consumers — it simply was not enough to offset meat,
vegetables, bread and dairy, which together are six times fruit's weight.

**Fresh fruit and vegetables are only 23.5% of the food basket.** The other 76.5%
is processed food, livestock products and beverages, where the orchard is a minor
share of cost and energy, feed, packaging, labour and rent dominate.

## 7. Vegetables: the mirror image

Vegetables are the opposite case and they explain why "flat production" still hurt:

- Production: **−0.8%** (flat)
- Farm-gate price: **+61.1%** y/y — the largest rise of any agricultural sub-group
- Retail price: **+39.6%** y/y

Here the farmer did well and the retailer's margin *compressed* by 21 points. The
+5.58pp that vegetables contribute to food inflation is, unusually, a genuine
farm-level price event — flat supply against a growing, higher-cost economy.

## 8. Is the widening gap a long-run trend?

Testing seven commodities where a farm-gate price and a matching retail category
both exist, 2018 → 2024:

| Commodity | Farm | Retail | Margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Onions | +777% | +794% | −3.8% |
| Raw cow's milk | +900% | +848% | −5.1% |
| Potatoes | +793% | +585% | −23.4% |
| Wheat → flour | +852% | +615% | −24.8% |
| Tomatoes | +1012% | +525% | −41.2% |
| Apples | +855% | +457% | −41.2% |
| Oranges | +752% | +373% | −44.0% |

In **every single pair**, farm-gate prices rose *faster* than the matching retail
price over 2018-2024. Percentage marketing margins narrowed, they did not widen.
Over 2010-2025 the retail/farm ratio drifted up only ~25% in total (~1.5% a year),
and farmers' real terms of trade actually *improved* (+9%).

So there is no long-running secular squeeze in the aggregate data. **2026 is a
break from the pattern, not the continuation of one** — and the break is dominated
by one fact: a 60% fruit harvest crashed the farm index while every non-farm cost
in the economy rose 25-45%.

## 9. A measurement caveat that matters

Part of the headline "farm +12.9% vs retail +27%" gap is a **composition artefact,
not a margin**. Tarım-ÜFE weights fruit heavily, and fruit prices collapsed on a
record harvest; the food CPI weights meat, bread and dairy heavily, and those are
driven by imported feed and energy. The two indices are moving apart partly because
they measure different things. The per-commodity tests in §8 and the stage tests in
§5 exist precisely to avoid resting the conclusion on that comparison.

## What this adds up to

1. **The farmer's squeeze is real**: output +12.9%, inputs +30.3%, terms of trade −13.3%.
2. **It is mostly not a transfer to traders or supermarkets.** Retail food at +26.9%
   is below the minimum wage, rent, packaging, utilities and every imported input in
   lira. The gap opens between farm and *factory*, not factory and shelf.
3. **The rent, to the extent there is one, is collected upstream and outside
   agriculture** — by energy, by the exchange rate acting on imported feed and grain,
   by administered utility prices, by commercial landlords, and by the wage bill.
4. **Two genuine margin expansions are visible**: meat at retail (+9.1pp over
   processing) and dairy at processing (+42.5%, the fastest in the chain). Both are
   under active competition investigation.
5. **Your intuition about fruit was right about the price and wrong about the
   victim**: traders did pay farmers less, but they passed the lower price on —
   retail fruit fell 1.0%. The farmer absorbed a normal supply-driven price
   collapse while their input bill rose 30%. That is the injustice in the data,
   and it is a *terms-of-trade* story, not a *margin* story.
